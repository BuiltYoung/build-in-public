# Performance review

The goal is to find what this founder's audience responds to and adjust the plan, not to grade the posts. Work only from numbers the user gives you.

## What to ask for

Accept whatever they have: a pasted table, platform screenshots, or rough notes. Ideally, per post:

- Platform, date and time, pillar, format (text, image, carousel, video)
- Reach or impressions
- Reactions, comments, shares or reposts, saves
- Profile visits, new followers
- Link clicks, DMs, sign-ups, orders, enquiries that came from it

Don't make them collect everything before you help. Work with what's there and name the one or two numbers that would most improve the next review.

## Be honest about sample size

With fewer than about 10 posts on a platform, or very small reach, patterns are weak. Call them early signals, not conclusions, and suggest what to test next. Founders reshape their whole plan around one lucky post more often than they should.

## Metrics by goal

| Goal | Look at | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Getting seen | Reach, impressions, new followers | Whether the hook and format get the post shown |
| Resonance | Comments, saves and shares relative to reach | People who comment, save or share cared; a like is cheap |
| Business results | DMs, sign-ups, orders, enquiries | The reason the founder is posting at all |

Engagement rate = (reactions + comments + shares + saves) ÷ reach (or impressions). Use whichever base the platform gives, and keep it consistent across posts.

For build in public, DMs and comments from potential customers often matter more than any public number. Ask about them.

## Compare against the founder's own baseline

Compare each post with the founder's median on the same platform, not with industry averages. Benchmarks vary hugely by audience size, industry and platform, and most published figures aren't comparable. Never state an industry benchmark unless the user provided it or you found it with a cited source.

## Diagnosing patterns

| Pattern | Likely cause | What to try |
|---|---|---|
| Low reach, strong engagement rate | The topic works; distribution doesn't | Sharper hook, a different format, a different posting window |
| High reach, weak engagement | The hook promised more than the post delivered, or the topic is too broad | Make the post deliver the hook's promise; narrow the topic |
| Strong engagement, no business results | No path from post to product | A clearer next step: profile link, waitlist, "DM me", a pinned post |
| One pillar consistently above the median | The audience wants more of it | Shift the pillar mix towards it |
| A platform consistently below the median after 8 to 10 posts | Wrong platform, or the wrong format for it | Change the format first; then consider dropping the platform |

Label every cause as a hypothesis ("likely", "worth testing"), not a finding.

## Output format

1. **What worked:** two or three posts, with the numbers that show it
2. **What didn't:** with numbers
3. **Hypotheses:** clearly labelled as such
4. **Three changes for the next 2 to 4 weeks:** specific (for example: "move decision posts to LinkedIn; test a carousel version of the best Instagram post")
5. **What to track next time:** one or two numbers they don't have yet
6. **Updated profile:** add a short "what's working" note to it

## Running experiments

Run one experiment per platform at a time (hook style, format, posting window or pillar) and keep it for 2 to 4 weeks before judging. Changing everything at once makes it impossible to tell what worked. Fixing an obvious gap (a missing next step for buyers, an unreported poll result) isn't an experiment and can happen alongside. When the three changes include an experiment, say which one it is and what it will be measured against.
