# Content calendar

A calendar the founder abandons in week three is worse than a small one they keep. Size it to their real time first, then fill it.

## 1. Cadence from available time

If they haven't said how much time they have, use the default of 3 posts a week and say so.

| Time per week | Cadence | Platforms |
|---|---|---|
| About 1 hour | 2 posts | 1 |
| 2 to 3 hours | 3 to 4 posts | 2 (one home platform, one adapted) |
| 5+ hours | Short posts most days plus 1 to 2 longer pieces | 2 to 3, plus a community space |

## 2. Pillar mix by stage

Starting points, not rules. Early stages have no customers to feature but plenty of decisions; later stages have proof. Adjust after a performance review shows what the audience responds to.

| Stage | Milestones | Decisions | Failures | Customers | Behind the scenes | Invitations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Idea | 5% | 30% | 15% | 10% (interview quotes) | 10% | 30% |
| Building | 15% | 25% | 15% | 10% | 20% | 15% |
| Beta | 15% | 20% | 15% | 20% | 15% | 15% |
| Just launched | 25% | 15% | 10% | 25% | 10% | 15% |
| Growth | 20% | 20% | 10% | 25% | 10% | 15% |

With only a few posts a week, turn percentages into a rotation: over a month, make sure each pillar appears and the biggest ones appear more than once.

## 3. Platform allocation and repurposing

Pick one **home platform** where a story first appears in full. Other platforms get an adapted version, not a copy. Each platform's audience notices cross-posts, and formats differ enough that a pasted post usually looks wrong.

One story, several shapes:
- LinkedIn: the full story with the reasoning
- X: the sharpest single line plus the number, or a short thread
- Instagram: a carousel (problem, attempt, result) or a short video
- TikTok: a 30 to 60 second talking-head or process clip
- 小红书: a note with a cover image that states the number or result in large text
- Community group: a follow-up question that invites members into the next decision

## 4. Weekly rhythm

A fixed rhythm helps because it turns posting into a habit; the exact days matter less than keeping them. An example for 3 posts a week:

- **Early week:** a decision or behind-the-scenes post (what's happening this week)
- **Midweek:** the main story (milestone, failure retro, or customer story)
- **End of week:** an invitation or poll, plus a short recap in the community space

Keep one evergreen post in reserve (a behind-the-scenes piece or a lesson) for weeks when nothing happens.

## 5. Calendar format

Deliver as a table in the conversation. Offer a CSV if they want to import it somewhere.

| Date | Platform | Pillar | Story / angle | Concrete detail (from the user) | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tue 4 Mar | LinkedIn | Decision | Why we dropped the free plan | 40 free users, 3 converted | Ready | |
| Thu 6 Mar | Instagram | Behind the scenes | Packing day | needs: photo of packing table, number of orders packed | Idea | |
| Fri 7 Mar | WhatsApp group | Invitation | Vote on next scent | 2 options from the user | Draft | Report results next Fri |

**Only fill the "concrete detail" column with things the user gave you.** For slots without material, write `needs:` followed by exactly what to collect. That turns the calendar into a collection list instead of an invitation to make things up. Status values: Idea, Needs material, Draft, Ready, Posted.

## 6. Story bank

Keep unused story ideas as one-liners in the profile's story bank. When planning, pull from the bank first, and move stories into the story log once they are posted so they don't repeat.
