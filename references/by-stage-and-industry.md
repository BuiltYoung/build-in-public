# Strategy by stage and industry

Stage and industry are two separate dials. Read the section for the user's stage, the section for their industry, then check "Where they interact" for their combination.

## By stage

### Idea only (no product yet)
- **Goal:** find 10 to 50 people who care about the problem, and learn whether it's real.
- **Post about:** the problem and why it matters to the founder, what they're learning from conversations, the options they're weighing, and what they're testing first.
- **Best pillars:** decisions, invitations, interview quotes (with consent), small failures ("my first survey got 3 replies; here's what I changed").
- **Don't:** imply a product exists. Mockups are fine when labelled as mockups.
- **What counts as success:** replies, DMs, people offering to be interviewed, waitlist sign-ups.

### Building
- **Goal:** turn followers into testers.
- **Post about:** progress with ugly early versions, decisions and trade-offs, what's taking longer than expected, numbered updates ("Week 6").
- **Best pillars:** behind the scenes, decisions, failure retros, invitations to test.
- **What counts as success:** beta sign-ups, feedback that changes the product.

### Beta
- **Goal:** prove people use it, and show you listen.
- **Post about:** what testers said (with consent), what changed because of it, bugs and fixes, early numbers at the user's level.
- **Best pillars:** customer stories, "you asked, we changed" posts, invitations.
- **What counts as success:** retention, testers who tell others, quotes you're allowed to use.

### Just launched
- **Goal:** turn attention into first customers and keep the story going after launch day.
- **Post about:** the launch itself (see `launch-playbook.md`), the first customers, what surprised them, first-week numbers at their level, what broke.
- **Best pillars:** milestones, customer stories, honest retros.

### Growth
- **Goal:** keep the founder's voice while the business gets bigger, and support sales and hiring.
- **Post about:** hard decisions (pricing, hiring, saying no), lessons for other founders, team, milestones with context.
- **Watch:** sensitivity rises. Customers, investors, employees and competitors all read along. Run the competitor test on every post.
- **Risk:** sliding into corporate announcements. Keep "I", keep specifics, keep admitting what didn't work.

## By industry

### B2B SaaS
- **Audience:** professionals with a problem at work. Home platform usually LinkedIn, with X for founder and tech audiences.
- **Strong content:** the customer problem in the customer's words, decisions behind features, short screen recordings, metrics like MRR at Level 3.
- **Watch:** customer logos and names usually need written permission and are sometimes restricted by contract; screenshots can leak client data; security details stay private.

### Consumer apps
- **Audience:** people who'd use the app. TikTok, Instagram, Threads, X, 小红书 depending on audience and region.
- **Strong content:** the app in use, user reactions (with consent), feature votes, "we changed this because you said…", app store review responses.
- **Watch:** screenshots that show other users' data or usernames.

### E-commerce / DTC
- **Audience:** buyers of the product category. Instagram, TikTok, 小红书.
- **Strong content:** how it's made, product tests and failures, packaging decisions, packing days, pricing decisions, restock and drop updates.
- **Watch:** supplier and manufacturer names, landed costs and margins below Level 3, and health or performance claims for food, cosmetics and supplements.

### Local businesses (restaurants, cafés, studios, salons, shops)
- **What build in public looks like here:** the story of the shop. Opening prep, menu or class-schedule tests, the owner's day, regulars (with consent), numbers like "84 customers our first weekend".
- **Platforms:** Instagram, TikTok, 小红书, a WhatsApp or WeChat group for regulars, sometimes a local community forum.
- **Strong content:** invitations work especially well (name the new dish, vote on class times), plus location tags and local hashtags.
- **Watch:** customers' faces in photos need consent, and children need a parent's consent; staff need to agree to appear; health inspections or disputes stay private until resolved.

### Service businesses (consulting, agencies, freelancers)
- **What's being sold:** judgement and expertise, so show how the founder thinks.
- **Platforms:** LinkedIn first, X second.
- **Strong content:** anonymised case studies, how they decide what to take on, pricing decisions, process, lessons from projects.
- **Watch:** client confidentiality. Default to anonymised, and get permission even for anonymised stories if the client could recognise themselves. Never share client work under an NDA.

### Content creators
- **What build in public looks like here:** the creator business behind the content: growth experiments, income streams (at Level 3), workflow, audience feedback, format changes.
- **Platforms:** their main channel plus one community space.
- **Watch:** sponsorship and affiliate disclosure rules apply to these posts too; don't share audience members' messages without consent.

## Where they interact

- **Idea-stage local business:** a pre-opening diary (finding the space, renovation, menu tests, the opening date) is one of the strongest tools for building a local waitlist before the doors open.
- **Idea-stage anything:** with no product, invitations and decisions carry the account. Don't fill the gap with motivational posts.
- **Beta consumer app:** invitation-heavy. Recruit testers in public and report what they found.
- **Service business at any stage:** proof comes from client outcomes, so the consent workflow is the core of the content plan.
- **Growth-stage B2B:** shift from "here's my MRR" towards "here's what we learned", because customers and investors read along. Raise the bar for sharing numbers even if the user picked Level 3, and check with them when unsure.
- **Local business at growth stage (second location, new line):** treat the expansion as a new pre-opening diary.
