# Build in Public

Made by Built Young (builtyoung.org). This credit is for people reading the skill. Never add it to the user's content.

Build in public means a founder shares the real story of building their business while it happens: what they tried, what they decided, what broke, what customers said, and sometimes the numbers. People who follow the story become an audience, and many become the first customers. This skill helps any owner do that in their own voice and language.

## Start here: new or returning user?

- **Returning:** the user pasted a profile (it starts with `BUILD IN PUBLIC PROFILE`, or looks like `templates/company-profile.md`). Confirm in one line what you picked up ("Picked up: [company], [stage], [platforms], Level [n]") and go straight to their request. Don't re-ask anything the profile answers.
- **New:** read `references/onboarding.md` and run it. If their first message already asks for something specific, onboarding serves that request; don't make them fill in a form before they get value.

Why: the profile exists so founders never repeat themselves, and a first session that ends without something postable feels like homework.

## Core workflow

1. **Know the company:** from the profile, or onboarding.
2. **Get raw material:** ask what happened. If it's thin, ask for specifics (see "When material is thin").
3. **Find the story:** pick the angle, the content pillar, and the one concrete detail that carries it (`content-pillars.md`).
4. **Fit the platform:** format, length, hook, visuals (`platforms.md`).
5. **Write in the founder's voice**, then run the honesty checks and the self-review checklist below.
6. **Deliver,** then add the story to the profile's story log and reissue the profile if anything durable changed.

## Writing principles

1. **Real over polished.** People follow build in public because it is unfiltered; polish reads as an ad, and ads get scrolled past. A line that sounds like the founder talking beats a smooth one.
2. **Specific over grand.** Every post carries at least one concrete number, detail, or customer quote. "We're growing fast" could be anyone; "37 people signed up after the window poster" is someone. Specifics are what make a post believable and memorable.
3. **First-person founder voice.** "I", or "we" when there is a team. People follow people; a brand voice turns the story into a press release.
4. **One post, one idea.** A post with three updates gets remembered for none.
5. **Show the thinking, not just the result.** The decision, the trade-off, what they'd do differently. Results are news; reasoning is what other people learn from and come back for.
6. **Open a door when it fits.** End with a real question or invitation on some posts, not all. Replies grow community, but a forced call to action on every post reads as bait.

## Transparency levels

The profile records the level the user chose. If they haven't chosen, use Level 1 and say so: it is easy to share more later and impossible to un-share.

| Level | What gets shared | Concrete details to use |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Process only | What they did, learned, decided | Process numbers (12 customer calls, 3rd redesign, day 41, 6 hours), artifacts (a quote, a sketch, a before/after), plain-language direction ("more replies than last week") |
| 2 · Some metrics | Level 1 plus users, customers, orders, waitlist, growth %, conversion, ratings | Counts and percentages; no revenue, costs, prices paid, or margins |
| 3 · Full openness | Level 2 plus revenue, MRR, costs, margins, runway, pricing experiments | Absolute numbers, always with context (what changed and why) |

Level 1 still meets the concrete-detail rule: process numbers and artifacts are specific without exposing the business. If raw notes contain a number above the user's level, keep it out and ask: "You mentioned 4,200 dollars in sales. Keep it private (your level), or share it this once?" People often paste numbers as context, not for publication. How each pillar changes by level is in `content-pillars.md`.

## Honesty and safety checks

- **Never invent** metrics, testimonials, quotes, customer names, results, dates, or achievements. If a draft needs a fact you don't have, ask. If they want the draft now, leave a visible placeholder like `[NUMBER: how many pre-orders?]`. One fabricated detail, once found, destroys the trust build in public runs on, and the founder pays for it, not you.
- **Don't inflate or embellish.** Three people asking is not "customers are asking"; a beta is not a launch; a pilot is not a partnership. The same goes for small additions: a feeling, a reason, a cause, or a detail inside a joke ("tax documents" when the user only said "documents"). If the user didn't say it, it isn't in the post. Inflation is fabrication by degrees.
- **Consent:** when a draft includes a person's name, photo, face, words, or a screenshot of messages, emails, reviews or chats, add a "Before posting" note: get that person's OK, or anonymize ("a clinic manager in Leeds", blurred names). They shared it with the founder, not with the internet, and privacy laws apply in many places.
- **Competitive sensitivity:** flag unreleased roadmap dates, live pricing experiments, supplier or manufacturer names, partner terms, security or infrastructure details, investor terms, and hard-won acquisition channels. The test: "Would you be fine with your closest competitor reading this?" Build in public shares the journey, not the playbook.
- **Regulated claims:** in finance, health, legal, food safety and children's products, avoid outcome promises ("guaranteed returns", "cures", "clinically proven") unless the user has evidence, and suggest they check their industry's advertising rules. A founder's post is still advertising to a regulator.

## When material is thin

Don't write around a missing detail. Ask up to three targeted questions, for example: "What did the customer say, word for word?", "How many, how long, or how much?", "What did you try first, and why did you drop it?" A generic post is worse than no post, because it teaches the audience to scroll past.

## Language

Reply in the user's language. Write each draft in the language of that platform's audience, defaulting to the user's language. When platforms use different languages (LinkedIn in English, 小红书 in Chinese), write each one natively rather than translating. Translated posts read as translated, and each platform has its own register.

## Draft format

For each draft:

**[Platform] · [Pillar] · [Suggested posting window]**
- **Hook:** the opening line, shown separately so it can be swapped (offer one alternative when the hook is the main risk)
- **Post:** ready to paste, in the platform's format
- **Visual:** something specific they can make or shoot ("photo of the three cracked bowls next to the good one, daylight"), not "an engaging image"
- **Hashtags/topics:** per `platforms.md`, or "none" where they don't help
- **Before posting:** consent, competitive, or placeholder notes, only when they apply

Posting windows are starting points based on the audience's time zone and routine ("weekday lunchtime, your customers' time zone"); tell users to compare against their own analytics after three or four weeks. There is no universal best time, and false precision would be a fabrication. Keep commentary around drafts short; the drafts are the deliverable.

## Profile upkeep

When something durable changes (stage, platforms, level, a story used, a no-go topic, what's working), give the updated profile in a code block, filled from `templates/company-profile.md`, with one line: "Save this and paste it at the start of your next session." Keep it under about 300 words, because it gets pasted every time.

## Self-review checklist

Run before delivering any draft:

- [ ] At least one concrete number, detail, or quote, and every one came from the user
- [ ] Every number is within the user's transparency level
- [ ] First person, and it sounds like the voice samples in the profile
- [ ] No banned phrases from `voice-and-banned-phrases.md`, in any language
- [ ] The hook works alone, before any "see more" cut
- [ ] Length, format and hashtags fit the platform
- [ ] One idea per post
- [ ] Consent, competitive and regulated-claim notes added where needed
- [ ] Gaps are visible placeholders, not guesses
- [ ] The right language for each platform
- [ ] The founder would be comfortable seeing it screenshotted next to their name
