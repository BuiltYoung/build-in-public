# Platforms

Limits and features below were checked in October 2026. Platforms change them often, so treat numbers as current conventions, and if web search is available, verify a limit before relying on it in a tight spot. Posting windows are starting points to test against the user's own analytics, never facts.

## Quick reference

| Platform | Best for | Length | Hashtags / topics | Starting cadence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| X | Fast, frequent updates; tech and founder audiences | 280 characters (longer for paid accounts) | 0 to 1 | 3 to 5 a week |
| LinkedIn | B2B, services, hiring, lessons | Up to 3,000 characters; cut at roughly 140 (mobile) to 210 (desktop) | 0 to 3 | 2 to 3 a week |
| Instagram | Visual products, local businesses, DTC | Caption up to 2,200 characters | Up to 5 (platform limit) | 3 to 4 posts a week plus Stories |
| TikTok | Consumer products, process, personality | Short video; caption secondary | 3 to 5 specific | 3 to 5 a week |
| 小红书 | Chinese-speaking consumers; searchable how-to and experience notes | Title about 20 characters; body up to about 1,000 | Several #话题 + keywords | 2 to 4 a week |
| Threads | Casual updates and questions | 500 characters | 1 topic tag | 3 to 5 a week |
| Reddit | Niche communities, honest feedback | Title up to 300 characters | None | Occasional, high-value |
| Discord | Owned community for engaged users | 2,000 characters per message | None | Founder present several times a week |
| WeChat groups | Chinese-speaking customers and early users | Short chat messages | None | 2 to 4 founder messages a week |
| WhatsApp | Customers and early users in many regions | Short chat messages | None | 1 to 3 messages a week |

## X

- **Format:** single posts for most updates; a thread (a chain of posts) for a full story. Images, short screen recordings and charts do well for build in public.
- **Hook:** the first line is the whole pitch. Lead with the number or the surprise.
- **Tone:** conversational, plain, a little informal. Short sentences.
- **Hashtags:** 0 to 1. They mostly add clutter here.
- **Build-in-public notes:** this is where the "week 12 update" and "today I learned" formats are most common. A screenshot of a chart or a real dashboard (within the user's level, with private details hidden) is strong.
- **Starting window:** weekday mornings or lunchtimes in the audience's time zone.

## LinkedIn

- **Format:** text post with short paragraphs and line breaks; an image, a document (a PDF carousel), or native video. Up to 3,000 characters, but most strong posts are far shorter.
- **Hook:** the first one or two lines must stand alone, because roughly 140 characters show on mobile before "see more".
- **Tone:** professional but personal. Lessons, decisions, and customer problems. Avoid motivational-poster writing and walls of emoji.
- **Hashtags:** 0 to 3 at the end.
- **Links:** some people put external links in the first comment instead of the post. Treat that as something to test, not a rule.
- **Build-in-public notes:** decision posts and failure retros work especially well with a professional audience. Customer stories need written consent; B2B customers often have contracts that restrict naming them.
- **Starting window:** weekday mornings in the audience's time zone.

## Instagram

- **Format:** feed posts (single image or carousel of up to 20 slides), Reels (short vertical video), Stories (24 hours; polls, question boxes and quizzes built in).
- **Hook:** the image or the first second of the Reel, then the first line of the caption, which shows before the cut.
- **Tone:** visual first; the caption tells the story behind the picture. Line breaks for readability.
- **Hashtags:** up to 5 per post (the current platform limit). Use specific ones (`#handmadeceramics`, `#[city]bakery`) over generic ones (`#business`). Local businesses should also add a location tag.
- **Build-in-public notes:** carousels suit "attempt 1, attempt 2, attempt 3" stories; Stories suit daily behind the scenes and quick polls; Reels suit process.
- **Starting window:** lunchtime and early evening in the audience's time zone.

## TikTok

- **Format:** vertical video. Talking head to camera, process clips, "Day X of building [thing]" series, or a voiceover over a screen recording or photos.
- **Hook:** the first one to three seconds, spoken and as on-screen text. Many people watch muted, so add on-screen captions.
- **Length:** 15 to 60 seconds suits most build-in-public updates; longer only when the story holds.
- **Tone:** personal, direct, unpolished. Phone-quality video is normal and expected.
- **Hashtags:** 3 to 5 specific ones; the caption can carry keywords people search for.
- **For drafts:** write a short script: hook line, 3 to 5 beats with what's on screen, closing line. Not a caption-only post.
- **Starting window:** evenings in the audience's time zone.

## Xiaohongshu (小红书 / RED)

- **Format:** image notes (a cover image plus several more) or video notes. The cover image matters most: large, clear text stating the result or the number ("开店30天 | 我们算错了3笔账").
- **Title:** about 20 characters. People search 小红书 like a search engine, so put the words customers would search for in the title and early in the body.
- **Body:** up to about 1,000 characters. Short paragraphs; emoji are commonly used as section markers. Experience-sharing tone (经验分享): what I did, what happened, what I'd tell you.
- **Topics:** several relevant #话题 at the end, mixing broad and specific (#创业日记 #烘焙 #多伦多美食 for a bakery).
- **Rules to respect:** avoid putting WeChat IDs, phone numbers or external links in notes; the platform restricts redirecting traffic elsewhere and may limit the note. Avoid absolute claims (最, 第一, 顶级); mainland China's advertising rules prohibit them in advertising, and the platform may flag them. Use comments and direct messages for contact.
- **Build-in-public notes:** "公开创业" and "创业日记" are understood framings. Behind-the-scenes, numbers-with-lessons, and "what I'd do differently" notes suit the platform.
- **Starting window:** lunchtime and evenings in the audience's time zone.

## Threads

- **Format:** short text posts up to 500 characters, with images or video. Replies and conversation carry most of the value.
- **Topics:** one topic tag per post.
- **Tone:** casual, conversational, more relaxed than LinkedIn. Questions work well.
- **Build-in-public notes:** good for quick updates, polls in question form, and testing an idea before writing it up properly elsewhere.

## Reddit

- **Rules first:** every community (subreddit) has its own rules, and many restrict self-promotion. Read the rules and recent posts before drafting. If the community bans promotion, don't write a disguised promotion.
- **Format:** a descriptive title (up to 300 characters) and a detailed text body. No hashtags.
- **Tone:** plain, humble, detailed, useful. Say up front that you're the founder. Marketing language gets downvoted.
- **What works:** genuinely useful write-ups (what we learned from X, here's the data), honest requests for feedback, and answering questions in the comments.
- **Never:** use second accounts or ask friends to upvote. Vote manipulation breaks site-wide rules and gets accounts banned.
- **Build-in-public notes:** participate in the community before posting about your own product. A founder with a comment history is read very differently from a new account that only posts its own link.

## Discord

- **Format:** a server with channels. Messages up to 2,000 characters (more on a paid tier). Native polls, threads, and events.
- **Starter structure:** #announcements (founder only), #introductions, #feedback, #show-and-tell or #wins, #general.
- **Tone:** casual, member-to-member. The founder is a participant, not a broadcaster.
- **For drafts:** write announcement posts, weekly prompts, and pinned welcome messages. See `community-playbook.md`.

## WeChat groups (微信群) and Moments (朋友圈)

- **Groups:** up to 500 members each. Use the group announcement (群公告) for rules and pinned info. Chatty Chinese voice; short messages, images, voice-message-length thoughts. Split a long update into two or three short messages rather than one wall of text. 接龙 works for sign-ups and quick counts.
- **Moments:** personal network updates, good for milestones and behind the scenes shared with people who know the founder.
- **Tone:** like talking to customers you know. 表情 and emoji are normal.
- **Avoid:** posting the same promotional message across many groups; members mute or leave, and group owners remove people for it.

## WhatsApp groups, communities and channels

- **Groups:** up to 1,024 members. Members can usually see each other's phone numbers, so tell people before adding them, and ask before adding anyone.
- **Communities:** an umbrella that holds several groups plus an announcement group; useful once there's more than one audience (customers, beta testers).
- **Channels:** one-way broadcasts. Followers don't see each other's numbers, which suits a public audience.
- **Tone:** short, friendly, personal. Native polls work in groups and channels.
- **Cadence:** fewer, more useful messages. Every message buzzes someone's phone.
