# Community playbook

Build in public turns into a business advantage when followers start talking back, and then start building with you. This file covers prompting conversation, polls, replying, involving early users, and running a community space.

## Questions that get answers

Specific questions get answers; open ones get silence. "Thoughts?" asks the reader to do all the work.

Patterns that work:
- **Either/or with stakes:** "Option A ships in May and costs less; option B ships now without dark mode. Which would you pick?"
- **Experience, not opinion:** "When did you last switch [tool or shop]? What made you leave?"
- **What would you do:** "A customer asked for a refund after 11 months of use. Policy says no. What would you do?"
- **Finish the sentence:** "The worst part of [task] is ___."
- **Your version:** "This is our packing table. What does yours look like?"
- **A small, concrete favour:** "Try the new checkout and tell me the step where you hesitated."

Rules: one question per post, at the end; make it answerable in under a minute; only ask what the founder will actually use.

## Polls

- **Use when:** there's a real decision with 2 to 4 clear options (names, flavours, features, class times, prices within the user's level).
- **Include:** one line on why you're asking and what you'll do with the result.
- **Close the loop:** post the result and what you did with it. A poll with no follow-up teaches people their votes don't matter.
- **Where:** native polls exist on X, LinkedIn, Instagram Stories, Threads, Discord and WhatsApp. Elsewhere, use "reply 1 or 2", or 接龙 in a WeChat group.

## Replying to comments

Reply promptly to early comments. The first conversations under a post set its tone, and on most platforms visible conversation helps a post travel further. Reply like a person, using the founder's voice, never a template that reads like one.

| Comment type | Approach |
|---|---|
| Praise | Thank them for something specific, then ask a follow-up ("Which part did you use first?") |
| A question | Answer fully. If several people ask, make it the next post |
| Fair criticism | Thank them, agree with what's true, and say what will change or why it won't. Don't defend by reflex |
| Hostile or unfair | One calm, factual reply, or none. Never argue in public. Hide or block abuse |
| Feature request | Say honestly whether and when. Log it; tag them back if it ships |
| Bug report in public | Thank them, move details to DMs, and post publicly when it's fixed |
| Competitor mention | Be gracious. Never criticise competitors |
| Spam or trolling | Don't engage |

Short reply starters (adapt to the founder's voice):
- "Thank you, that's exactly the kind of detail that helps. Can I ask what you were trying to do when it happened?"
- "Fair point. You're right about X. Y is staying for now because ___, but I'm watching it."
- "谢谢你说得这么具体！想再问一句：你当时是在哪一步卡住的？"
- "这个建议我们记下了，下个版本会试试，上线了第一时间告诉你。"

## Involving early users in building

People who help build something tell others about it. Ways to bring them in:

- **Beta or tester group:** a small group with early access and a direct line to the founder.
- **Naming and design votes:** product names, flavours, colours, packaging.
- **"You asked, we built":** credit the person who suggested a change (with their permission).
- **Founding members:** a name, badge or perk for the first 20 to 100 people, honoured for as long as promised.
- **Open feedback sessions:** a monthly call or live session where users watch a demo and react.
- **User spotlights:** a customer's story or work, with consent, in their own words.
- **Public changelog:** short, regular notes on what changed and why.

Close every loop. If you asked, report back. If you promised a perk, deliver it.

## Running a community space (Discord, WeChat, WhatsApp)

**When to start:** once roughly 10 to 20 people are already engaging (customers, testers, regular commenters). An empty group makes the first members feel they joined something dead.

**Set up:**
- A pinned welcome message: who it's for, what happens here, and the 2 or 3 rules.
- Discord: #announcements, #introductions, #feedback, #wins or #show-and-tell, #general.
- WeChat: 群公告 with rules and what the group is for; keep groups focused (customers and testers in separate groups).
- WhatsApp: a group for small, active circles; a Community with an announcement group for larger audiences; a Channel for one-way updates. Tell people that group members can see each other's phone numbers.

**Weekly rituals** (pick one or two and keep them):
- Start of week: "What are you working on / what do you need help with?"
- End of week: "One win, one thing that went wrong."
- Monthly: a founder update with numbers at the user's level, plus one decision members can vote on.

**Founder presence:** show up several times a week, reply personally, and ask more questions than you post announcements.

**Moderation:** clear rules, applied the same way to everyone. Remove spam quickly. Protect members' privacy: no sharing screenshots of member messages outside the group without permission.

## Signs a community is healthy

- Members reply to each other, not only to the founder
- Feedback from the group shows up in the product, and members notice
- New members arrive because existing members invited them
- A rough rule of thumb, not a benchmark: some members speak up each week, not only the same two or three
