# Launch playbook

A launch is a story with a date on it. Build-in-public founders have an advantage: people who followed the build already care how it ends. This file covers warm-up, launch day, and the two weeks after.

## Ground rules

- **No fake scarcity or fake countdowns.** "Only 20 founding spots" is fine if it's true and you'll honour it. "Almost sold out" when it isn't is a lie people remember.
- **No bought followers, likes, reviews or votes,** and no coordinated upvoting on platforms that forbid it. It breaks platform rules and gets accounts restricted, and fake numbers in a launch post are fabrication.
- **Supporters hear first.** Beta testers, the waitlist and community members should learn the date before the public does. They earned it.
- **Every launch post still follows the core rules:** first person, one concrete detail, within the user's transparency level.

## What "launch" means by business type

| Business | Launch looks like |
|---|---|
| B2B SaaS | Public availability, a pricing page, launch posts on LinkedIn and X, product directories and founder communities the audience uses |
| Consumer app | App store release, a TikTok and Instagram push, the beta community spreading the word |
| E-commerce / DTC | A drop or a first collection, pre-orders, a restock |
| Local business | A soft opening for friends and regulars, then an opening day or week, with a first-week special |
| Service business | A new offer, a first cohort, or opening a waitlist |
| Content creator | A paid tier, a course, a product, or a new show |

Launches are repeatable: a new feature, location, product line or price can each be its own smaller launch.

## Weeks 4 to 3 before: name the problem

- Post about the problem the product solves, in customers' words (with consent).
- Behind the scenes of the final build: what's left, what's hard.
- Open or push the waitlist with one clear action.
- Invitation: "What would make you try this on day one?"

## Weeks 2 to 1 before: show the thing, set the date

- Show real pieces of the product (screens, prototypes, the first batch, the finished space).
- Announce the date and where it will happen. Tell supporters first, then the public.
- Line up proof: tester quotes with permission, first-batch photos, demo clips.
- Prepare replies for the 5 questions people will ask (price, availability, how it differs, who it's for, what's next).
- Prepare launch-day assets in advance so the day is for replying, not making.

## Launch day

A simple order, adjusted to the audience's time zone:

1. **First thing:** a message to the community, waitlist and testers. "It's live. You helped build it. Here's the link." They should hear it from the founder before anyone else.
2. **Main post on the home platform:** what it is, who it's for, the one detail that makes it real (the number of testers, the 7th recipe attempt, the problem in a customer's words), and one clear action.
3. **Adapted posts on other platforms**, spaced out over the day rather than pasted at once.
4. **Reply all day.** This is where launch-day work actually happens.
5. **Midday or evening update:** one real number at the user's level ("43 sign-ups so far", "first 12 orders packed") or one surprising moment.
6. **End of day:** thank people specifically. Name what helped (with permission) and what happens next.

## Days 1 to 14 after

- **Day 1 or 2:** what happened, in numbers at the user's level, plus what surprised the founder.
- **First week:** the first customer stories (with consent), what broke and how it was fixed, the most common question answered.
- **Week 2:** a short retro: what they'd do again, what they wouldn't, what's next.
- **Throughout:** follow up personally with everyone who engaged, especially first customers.
- Keep the build-in-public rhythm going. The story didn't end at launch; it's the start of the next chapter.

## If the launch is quiet

Say so. A quiet launch is still a story, and an honest one: what they expected, what happened, what they think went wrong, what they'll try next. Readers trust founders who post this, and it often brings helpful advice. Don't hide it, and don't spin it.

## Launch-day checklist

- [ ] Supporters told first, with the date
- [ ] Main post and adapted posts drafted and checked against the self-review checklist
- [ ] Visuals ready
- [ ] Links tested; checkout or sign-up tested by someone outside the team
- [ ] Replies prepared for the 5 likely questions
- [ ] Consent confirmed for every quote, name and photo
- [ ] Time blocked to reply through the day
- [ ] Follow-up posts for day 1, week 1 and week 2 sketched, with `needs:` for the numbers you'll only know later
