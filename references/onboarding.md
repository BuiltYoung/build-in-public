# Onboarding

Goal: learn enough to write a good first post with at most five questions, fill everything else with defaults, and tell the user which defaults you used. The first session should end with a draft they can post, not just a questionnaire.

## Step 0: use what they already said

Before asking anything, pull out what the first message already tells you: the business, the audience, the stage, platforms, language, and any story ("we just got our first wholesale order"). Skip every question it answers. Asking someone to repeat themselves signals you weren't listening.

If the first message already gives you the business and a recent event with a concrete detail, don't hold the draft hostage to the questions: write the first draft now at Level 1 with defaults, then ask the remaining questions underneath it. Seeing a real draft also makes the questions easier to answer ("Level 2 would let us add how many you sold").

## If they don't know what build in public is

Explain it in one or two sentences in their language, then give one example from their industry. Don't lecture; the example does the explaining.

- **English:** "Build in public means sharing the real story of building your business as it happens: what you tried, what worked, what didn't, and sometimes the numbers. People who follow along often become your first customers."
- **中文:** "公开创业（build in public）就是把你做产品、做生意的真实过程边做边分享：试了什么、哪里做成了、哪里踩了坑，有时也包括数据。跟着看你过程的人，往往会成为你的第一批客户。"

One-line industry examples (all fictional; adapt to their details):

| Industry | Example post idea |
|---|---|
| B2B SaaS | "Week 6: 14 calls with clinic managers. 11 said scheduling isn't the problem, no-shows are. Rebuilding the dashboard around that." |
| Consumer app | "40% of beta users broke their streak on day 3. Testing a 'rest day' token this week." |
| E-commerce / DTC | "Third glaze test cracked in the kiln. Here's the before and after, and what I'm changing." |
| Local business | "We tried opening at 7am for two weeks. 9 customers on day one, 31 by day ten. Keeping it." |
| Service business | "I turned down a project this week. Here's how I decide what's a fit." |
| Content creator | "22 people unsubscribed after I changed the newsletter format. I read every reply." |

## The questions

Ask in one message, numbered, and say they can answer in any order or skip what they're unsure about.

1. **What do you sell, and to whom?** One sentence is enough. This sets the audience, which decides platform, tone and topics.
2. **Where are you right now (idea, building, beta, just launched, or growing), and what's one thing that happened recently?** The stage changes the whole strategy; the recent event becomes post number one.
3. **Where do you want to post?** "Not sure" is a fine answer; you will recommend two (see below).
4. **How much do you want to share?** Offer the three levels in plain words:
   - "Just the process: what I'm doing and learning, no business numbers"
   - "Some numbers: users, orders, growth, but not money"
   - "Everything, including revenue and costs"
5. **Optional:** "Paste something you've written recently, like a message to a friend about the business, so the posts sound like you." Skip this question if they seem rushed; you can learn the voice from their edits instead.

Don't ask about brand colours, competitor lists, long-term vision, follower counts or posting history at this stage. Each extra question costs momentum, and none of them is needed for a good first post.

## Recommending platforms when they're not sure

Recommend two at most: one where their customers already spend time, and one where showing work comes naturally. Two platforms posted weekly beat five posted once.

| Industry | Usual starting pair |
|---|---|
| B2B SaaS | LinkedIn + X (or a niche Reddit community) |
| Consumer app | TikTok or Instagram + X or Threads |
| E-commerce / DTC | Instagram + TikTok (小红书 for Chinese-speaking customers) |
| Local business | Instagram + a WhatsApp or WeChat customer group (小红书 where local customers use it) |
| Service business | LinkedIn (+ X) |
| Content creator | Their main channel + one community space |

Adjust for geography and language: Chinese-speaking audiences are often reached through 小红书 and WeChat rather than X.

## Defaults

Apply these unless the user says otherwise, and list the ones you used in a single line after the first draft ("Defaults I used: 3 posts a week, process-only sharing, first person, your time zone. Change any of these anytime.").

| Item | Default | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cadence | 3 posts a week across all platforms | Sustainable for a busy owner; consistency matters more than volume |
| Transparency | Level 1, process only | Easy to raise later, impossible to take back |
| Voice | First person "I"; "we" if they describe a team | People follow people |
| Language | The user's, per platform | Native writing reads better than translation |
| Time zone | The audience's; assume the founder's unless told otherwise | Posting windows depend on when the audience is around |
| Pillar mix | From `content-calendar.md` for their stage | Early stages have decisions to share; later stages have proof |
| Community space | None until there are people to put in it (roughly 10 to 20 engaged followers or customers) | An empty group makes the first members feel they joined something dead |

## After they answer

1. Read `by-stage-and-industry.md` for their stage and industry.
2. Write the first draft from the recent event they gave in question 2. If it lacks a concrete detail, ask one targeted follow-up instead of drafting around it.
3. List the defaults you used.
4. Give the filled-in profile from `templates/company-profile.md` with: "Save this and paste it at the start of your next session."
